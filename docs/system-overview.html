The core idea

The folder is the agency

This is the part worth understanding first, because everything else follows from it.

Every client gets a folder. Inside it, context/ holds what the team knows about that client, assets/ holds their real photos, and outputs/ holds the finished work. You open Claude in that folder and type a skill name.

No skill talks to another skill directly. Each one reads the context files it needs, does its job, and writes its result back as a file. That single rule is what makes the system reliable: any step can be re-run on its own, a session that ends mid-month loses nothing, and opening a different client folder gives you a team with a completely different brand in its head.

Why one folder per client matters

Brand guides, calendars and voice never bleed between clients, because they physically can't — they live in separate directories. Switching client is switching folder. And because everything is plain markdown, you can open any of it, read it, and edit it yourself without going through Claude at all.

Two more handoffs worth knowing

Alongside the context files, skills leave named fields inside their own output for a specific downstream specialist to pick up.

  • Visual Direction — every caption ends with one line describing the image it needs. The art director reads that line and generates from it, so the words and the picture always tell the same story.
  • Ready to schedule — every platform post is tagged with the platform, the intended slot, and whether it needs a supporting graphic. The publisher reads those tags and builds the schedule from them.

Foundation

stage 1–2 · sets everything else up
/brand-onboarding
Brand strategist · once per client

Visits the client's website and Instagram, screenshots both, and pulls out everything provable — their bio, product range, stated values, the words their own customers use in reviews, their dominant colours and typography. Then it sends the client a document that already has all of that filled in and only asks what genuinely can't be looked up: exact hex codes, fonts, who their ideal customer is, which products to push, what they'd never post. The answers become the brand guide.

/content-calendar
Strategist · start of each month

Blocks out the client's real events first — launches, promotions, seasonal moments — then balances the rest of the month. It sets how much of the month goes to each content pillar (and caps promotional content, because that's the mistake most businesses make), then how much goes to each format. Only after the shape is agreed does it fill the slots. Every slot names a specific topic and angle, never "educational post".

Images and video

stage 3 · the visual layer
/social-creative-designer
Art director

Four ways to make a post image. Generate builds a lifestyle scene from a concept. Composite takes the client's own product photo and builds a styled world around it. Brand adds the client's text treatment to a real photo and changes nothing else. Stop-motion renders a short looping Reel. Every prompt specifies subject, composition, action, location, style and the camera and lighting — it's written like a shot brief, because that's what gets a usable image.

In practice

What a month actually looks like

For a client already onboarded. Onboarding happens once, up front, and takes an hour.

Day 1
Open the client's folder, type /social-media-manager. It reads the folder and tells you last month scored well on educational carousels and poorly on straight product posts. Run /content-calendar — it weights this month accordingly, blocks out the two promotions you added to upcoming-events.md, and briefs sixteen slots. You read the summary table and move two posts. Approved.
Day 1–2
Content production. /repurpose takes the month's three strongest angles and fans each one out. Then the specialists run: captions for Instagram and Facebook, native posts for LinkedIn, Threads and X, two carousels, four reel scripts. Everything lands in outputs/ as markdown you can read, edit or paste anywhere. You review each batch before the next starts.
Day 2–3
Visuals. /social-creative-designer works one post at a time, reading the Visual Direction the copywriter left. Product posts use the client's real product photograph inside a generated scene. /carousel-designer builds the two carousels as locked panel sets. You approve each image as it comes.
Day 3
/publisher lays out the full month — every post, its platform, its date and time, its image. You scan it once, adjust two times, confirm. The month goes live on schedule. That's the month's production done in three days.
Throughout
/community-manager drafts replies to comments and DMs in the brand's voice, sorted so you only handle the ones that need a human. Ten minutes a day instead of an hour.
End of month
/social-performance-review pulls the numbers and produces a client-ready report — what worked, what didn't, and what to change. It writes the findings back into the folder, and next month's calendar starts from them. The loop closes, and month three is better than month one.

You are in the loop at every gate

This is deliberate, not a limitation. The system produces the work; a person decides whether it ships. Five sign-off points — the brand guide, the calendar, each content batch, each visual, and the full schedule — and nothing passes one without you.
